Project Overview

Glass Fiber Reinforced Concrete (GFRC) is heavily used in modern construction cladding due to its high tensile strength. However, the manufacturing process generates significant scrap waste that is typically sent to landfills. This project investigated recycling GFRC factory waste by crushing it back into aggregate sizes and blending it into secondary binder structures. Using Finite Element Method (FEM) simulation and physical lab testing, we proved the recycled material's structural load limits for architectural paving applications.

Key Deliverables

  • Developed inhomogeneous, composite material property cards in SolidWorks Simulation to represent the distributed glass fiber clusters.
  • Conducted mechanical shear, static loading, and buckling FEM simulations on architectural paving blocks.
  • Performed physical three-point bending tests on an MTS Universal Testing Machine to chart real flexural strength limits.
  • Extracted load limits and Stress-Strain curves, comparing them to control samples (plain concrete without fiber content).
  • Validated finite element numerical outputs against classical Euler-Bernoulli beam formulas, achieving a strong 95% correlation match.
  • Certified "Fydro Baking Stones" for load paths up to 15kN, demonstrating structural safety and commercial viability.
