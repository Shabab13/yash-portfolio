Project Overview

The CORDS VTS project aims to solve a major research challenge in urban traffic safety: simulating the specific impact kinetics of cyclists in collisions. Standard automotive crash test dummies are rigid and expensive, failing to represent the body dynamics and rotational kinematics of a bicycle rider. This project developed a cost-effective, articulated mechatronic dummy system using smart materials and 3D printing.

Key Deliverables

  • Designed 12 interlocking joint segments in SolidWorks adhering to human skeletal range-of-motion constraints.
  • Optimized FDM 3D print orientations and infill densities in Cura, achieving a 30% reduction in print material and zero overhang failures.
  • Iterated prototypes through laser-cut EVA foam skin layer integration to represent soft-tissue damping.
  • Developed magnetic quick-connect joints to simulate joint dislocations and breakaway actions during high-velocity impacts.
  • Integrated sensor brackets accommodating three-axis accelerometers to collect real-time impact acceleration curves.
  • Conducted 24 physical test drops, correlating telemetry data with simulated impact profiles.
